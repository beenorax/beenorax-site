Editorial and Publishing Policies

Our editorial and publishing framework establishes the principles of research integrity, editorial independence, transparency, responsible scholarship and ethical publication for Beenorax journals.

Policy framework · Version 1.0 · 21 November 2026
Important publication status notice

Beenorax Journal of Biomedical Sciences (BJBS) and Beenorax Journal of Business Analytics (BJBA) are new scholarly publishing initiatives under development by Beenorax Analytics & Consulting.

Online open-access publication is planned. Editorial appointments, peer-review procedures, author instructions, licensing terms, publication charges and digital preservation arrangements must be confirmed before formal manuscript submissions open.

The policies on this page constitute a proposed framework pending formal approval and operational implementation.

1. Editorial governance and independence

Beenorax Analytics & Consulting is the publisher of BJBS and BJBA. The publisher provides administrative, technical and publishing support while maintaining the independence of editorial decisions.

  • Each journal will have its own Editor-in-Chief and editorial board.
  • Editorial appointments, affiliations and responsibilities will be published after confirmation.
  • Editorial decisions will be based on scholarly merit, relevance, originality, methodological quality and ethical compliance.
  • Commercial interests, advertising, sponsorship or financial considerations must not determine editorial decisions.
  • Editors must declare conflicts of interest and withdraw from decisions where impartiality may be compromised.
  • The publisher must not interfere improperly with independent editorial decisions.

2. Journal aims and scope

Beenorax Journal of Biomedical Sciences (BJBS)

BJBS is intended as a multidisciplinary journal covering the full spectrum of health and biomedical sciences. It welcomes basic, applied, clinical, translational, population-health and interdisciplinary research.

Its subject areas include, but are not limited to:

  • Medicine and clinical specialties.
  • Medical laboratory sciences and laboratory medicine.
  • Haematology and transfusion medicine.
  • Clinical chemistry, pathology and metabolic sciences.
  • Microbiology, parasitology and infectious diseases.
  • Immunology and immunopathology.
  • Pharmacology, pharmaceutical sciences and medicinal plants.
  • Nursing, midwifery and patient care sciences.
  • Dentistry and oral health.
  • Public health, epidemiology and global health.
  • Nutrition and dietetics.
  • Physiotherapy, rehabilitation and allied health sciences.
  • Radiography and medical imaging.
  • Biomedical engineering and medical technologies.
  • Molecular biology, cell biology and biochemistry.
  • Genetics, genomics and molecular medicine.
  • Biotechnology, bioinformatics and computational biology.
  • Health informatics and digital health.
  • Environmental and occupational health.
  • Veterinary and comparative biomedical sciences.
  • Other relevant areas of health and biomedical sciences.

Beenorax Journal of Business Analytics (BJBA)

BJBA is intended to publish scholarly research in business analytics, quantitative methods, computational sciences, management and evidence-based decision-making.

Its subject areas include:

  • Business intelligence and data analytics.
  • Artificial intelligence and machine learning.
  • Applied statistics and data science.
  • Financial, accounting and investment analytics.
  • Marketing and customer analytics.
  • Operations and supply chain analytics.
  • Human resource analytics.
  • Economics, entrepreneurship and innovation.
  • Business management and organisational research.
  • Information systems and digital transformation.
  • Risk analysis, forecasting and decision sciences.

Article types

Subject to final journal-specific author instructions, the journals may consider:

  • Original research articles.
  • Systematic and narrative reviews.
  • Meta-analyses.
  • Short communications and brief reports.
  • Case reports and case series, where appropriate.
  • Methods and technical papers.
  • Letters to the editor.
  • Perspectives and commentaries.
  • Editorials.

3. Editorial assessment and peer review

Manuscripts submitted after the journals officially open will undergo editorial assessment and independent peer review according to the published procedures of the relevant journal.

Initial editorial assessment

  • Submissions will be assessed for relevance to the journal's scope.
  • Editors will examine originality, scientific quality, completeness and ethical compliance.
  • Manuscripts may be rejected before external review where they do not meet basic requirements.

External peer review

  • Suitable manuscripts will be sent to independent reviewers with relevant subject expertise.
  • Reviewers must maintain confidentiality and declare competing interests.
  • Reviewers should provide objective, constructive and evidence-based assessments.
  • Authors may be required to revise their manuscripts in response to reviewer comments.
  • Final editorial decisions will remain the responsibility of authorised journal editors.

The final peer-review model, reviewer requirements, number of reviewers and decision procedures will be published before formal submissions open.

4. Research and publication ethics

Authors, editors and reviewers are expected to uphold accepted principles of research integrity and responsible scholarly publishing.

Research submitted for publication must be original, scientifically valid, honestly reported and conducted in accordance with applicable ethical and regulatory requirements.

The journals will not knowingly publish work involving fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, unethical research practices or deliberate misrepresentation of findings.

5. Authorship and contributorship

Authorship must reflect meaningful intellectual or scientific contributions to the research and manuscript.

  • All listed authors must meet recognised authorship criteria.
  • All authors must approve the final manuscript.
  • Each author must accept accountability for their contribution.
  • Individuals who do not meet authorship criteria should be acknowledged appropriately, with permission.
  • Gift, guest and ghost authorship are unacceptable.
  • Changes to authorship after submission must be explained and agreed to by all affected authors.
  • Corresponding authors are responsible for coordinating communication with the editorial office.

Contributor roles may be described using the CRediT taxonomy where appropriate.

6. Conflicts of interest and funding disclosure

Authors, editors and reviewers must disclose financial, professional, institutional or personal relationships that could influence or appear to influence their work or editorial judgement.

Authors must disclose:

  • Research grants and funding sources.
  • Financial relationships relevant to the manuscript.
  • Relevant institutional or professional affiliations.
  • Personal or professional relationships that may create competing interests.

Editors and reviewers must declare relevant conflicts and withdraw from affected editorial or review activities where necessary.

Funding sources must be accurately reported. Funders must not be allowed to improperly influence research findings or publication decisions.

7. Research ethics, consent and clinical registration

Research involving human participants, identifiable human data, human biological materials or animals must comply with applicable ethical standards and regulatory requirements.

Human research

  • Authors must provide relevant ethics committee or institutional review approval details where required.
  • Research involving human participants must follow appropriate informed-consent requirements.
  • Authors must protect participant confidentiality and personal information.
  • Identifiable photographs, clinical records or other personal information require appropriate consent for publication.
  • Ethical approval must not be claimed where it was not obtained.

Animal research

Animal studies must comply with applicable institutional and national animal welfare requirements. Authors should report relevant ethical approval and procedures used to minimise animal suffering.

Clinical trials

Clinical trials must be registered in an appropriate public registry where required by applicable standards. Registration details should be included in the manuscript.

8. Plagiarism, originality and duplicate publication

Submitted manuscripts must represent original scholarly work and must not involve plagiarism, substantial unattributed text reuse, fabricated references or misleading presentation of previously published material.

  • All sources must be appropriately acknowledged.
  • Direct quotations must be identified and referenced.
  • Previously published work must be disclosed where relevant.
  • Simultaneous submission of the same manuscript to multiple journals is not permitted.
  • Redundant or duplicate publication must be disclosed.
  • Text recycling will be assessed in context, including whether it misleads readers or breaches publication ethics.
  • Image manipulation or alteration intended to misrepresent research findings is unacceptable.

The journals intend to establish appropriate originality-screening procedures before formal submissions open.

9. Artificial intelligence and automated tools

Authors remain fully responsible for the accuracy, originality, integrity and ethical compliance of submitted manuscripts.

  • AI tools must not be listed as authors.
  • Material use of generative AI in manuscript preparation should be disclosed.
  • Authors must verify AI-generated references, data, citations and scientific statements.
  • AI tools must not be used to fabricate research data, results, images or references.
  • Authors remain responsible for confidentiality and must not upload identifiable or confidential research information to external AI systems without appropriate authorisation.
  • Reviewers and editors must not upload confidential manuscripts or reviewer reports to AI systems without appropriate journal authorisation and safeguards.
  • AI assistance must not replace independent editorial judgement or scientific review.

The final author disclosure requirements will be specified in the journal-specific author instructions.

10. Data availability and research reproducibility

Authors should provide sufficient methodological information to allow readers to understand and evaluate their research.

  • Data supporting published findings should be made available where ethically and legally permissible.
  • Authors should identify appropriate repositories where datasets have been deposited.
  • Data availability statements should be included where applicable.
  • Confidential, sensitive or identifiable participant data must be protected.
  • Restrictions on data sharing should be explained transparently.
  • Analytical code, statistical methods and relevant research materials should be shared where appropriate.

11. Copyright, licensing and open access

Online open-access publication is planned for both Beenorax journals. The final copyright arrangements and open-access licence must be approved and publicly stated before formal submissions open.

The journals will clearly communicate:

  • Who retains copyright in published work.
  • The licence governing reuse and redistribution.
  • Author rights and repository deposit conditions.
  • Any restrictions on commercial or derivative reuse.
  • Applicable permissions for third-party material.

No specific Creative Commons licence should be assumed until formally selected and published.

12. Article processing charges and publication fees

Any article processing charges, submission fees or other publication costs must be approved and clearly disclosed before submissions open.

  • Fees must not be introduced without transparent public disclosure.
  • Authors must be informed of applicable charges before submission.
  • Any waiver or discount arrangements must be explained.
  • Payment must not guarantee acceptance or favourable peer review.
  • Editorial decisions must remain independent of payment.

Until the fee structure is formally approved, no claim that publication is free or fee-based should be made.

13. Corrections, expressions of concern and retractions

The journals aim to maintain an accurate and transparent scholarly record.

Where necessary, the journals may issue:

  • Corrections for errors that affect the accuracy or interpretation of published work.
  • Expressions of concern where serious questions remain unresolved.
  • Retractions where the reliability or integrity of an article is substantially compromised.
  • Editorial notices explaining significant changes to the publication record.

Decisions will be based on available evidence, relevant publication ethics guidance and appropriate editorial procedures. Published changes will be documented transparently.

14. Complaints and appeals

Authors may raise concerns about editorial decisions, peer review, publication procedures or alleged breaches of journal policies.

  • Complaints should provide sufficient information to allow investigation.
  • Appeals must explain the grounds for reconsideration.
  • Appeals will be assessed by an editor who was not responsible for the original decision where practicable.
  • Complaints involving the Editor-in-Chief should be referred to an appropriate independent publisher representative.
  • Retaliation against individuals raising legitimate concerns is unacceptable.

The official complaints contact and appeal procedure will be published before submissions open.

15. Research misconduct and investigations

Potential misconduct may include fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, unethical research, authorship manipulation, peer-review fraud, undisclosed competing interests or inappropriate image manipulation.

Where credible concerns arise, the journals may:

  • Assess the available evidence.
  • Request clarification or supporting documentation from authors.
  • Consult independent experts where necessary.
  • Contact relevant institutions, employers or funders where appropriate.
  • Pause editorial processing or publication where justified.
  • Issue corrections, expressions of concern or retractions where warranted.

Formal institutional investigations will generally remain the responsibility of the relevant research institutions and employers. The journals will avoid unsupported public accusations and will respond proportionately to available evidence.

16. Privacy, confidentiality and personal data

Personal information collected during manuscript submission, editorial processing and peer review must be handled responsibly and in accordance with applicable data protection requirements.

  • Author and reviewer information must be used for legitimate editorial purposes.
  • Unpublished manuscripts and reviewer reports must remain confidential.
  • Personal and sensitive research data must be protected.
  • Access to editorial information should be limited to authorised individuals.
  • Personal information must not be disclosed improperly.

A detailed privacy notice and data retention procedure must be published before formal submissions open.

17. Inclusion, accessibility and respectful conduct

The journals welcome scholarly contributions from researchers across disciplines, institutions, career stages and geographical regions.

  • Editorial decisions must be based on scholarly merit and published criteria.
  • Discrimination, harassment, intimidation and retaliation are unacceptable.
  • Authors, reviewers and editors must communicate respectfully.
  • Reasonable efforts should be made to improve website and submission accessibility.
  • Research must be presented accurately and respectfully, particularly where vulnerable populations are involved.

18. Special issues, supplements and guest editors

Special issues and supplements must comply with the same ethical and editorial standards as regular journal publications.

  • Guest editors must have appropriate subject expertise.
  • Guest editors must declare competing interests.
  • Manuscripts must undergo appropriate editorial assessment and peer review.
  • The Editor-in-Chief retains oversight of the integrity of the issue.
  • Funding and sponsorship arrangements must be disclosed.
  • Sponsors must not influence acceptance decisions or scientific conclusions.

19. Digital preservation and archiving

The publisher intends to establish appropriate digital preservation arrangements to support long-term access to published scholarly work.

The selected preservation service, repository arrangements and archiving procedures must be confirmed and tested before the journals claim to provide long-term digital preservation.

No archiving partnership or preservation service will be claimed without a confirmed arrangement.

20. Editorial workflow and operational readiness

Before formal manuscript submissions open, the publisher and each journal's editorial leadership must complete and test the following:

  • Appointment and confirmation of the Editor-in-Chief and editorial board.
  • Approval of journal-specific aims, scope and article types.
  • Publication of author instructions and reviewer guidance.
  • Selection and configuration of the peer-review model.
  • Establishment of secure manuscript submission and reviewer management procedures.
  • Approval of copyright, licensing and publication fee policies.
  • Establishment of ethics, consent and disclosure requirements.
  • Configuration of originality screening procedures.
  • Establishment of corrections, retractions, complaints and appeals procedures.
  • Publication of privacy and data protection notices.
  • Selection and testing of digital preservation arrangements.
  • Confirmation of ISSN application and DOI registration arrangements.
  • Testing of manuscript submission, review, editorial decisions and publication workflows.

21. Transparency and publication disclosures

The publisher and journals will provide accurate information about their ownership, editorial leadership, policies and publication arrangements.

The following information will be published when confirmed:

  • Publisher identity and contact information.
  • Journal editorial boards and institutional affiliations.
  • Peer-review procedures.
  • Copyright and licensing terms.
  • Publication charges or an approved no-fee statement.
  • Publication frequency and article processing procedures.
  • ISSN and DOI registration details.
  • Digital preservation arrangements.

The journals will not claim indexing, impact factors, accreditation, professional memberships or institutional partnerships unless independently confirmed.

22. Journal contacts and policy administration

The official editorial contact details for BJBS and BJBA will be published once the respective editorial offices are established.

Policy questions, complaints and appeals will be directed through the official contact channels published by the relevant journal.

These policies are subject to review and amendment as the journals develop. Approved changes will be documented with an effective date.

Current implementation status

The policy framework is available for review and formal approval. The journals remain in preparation. Formal submissions should not open until the editorial appointments, peer-review workflow, author instructions, licensing, fees, privacy arrangements and preservation procedures have been confirmed.
